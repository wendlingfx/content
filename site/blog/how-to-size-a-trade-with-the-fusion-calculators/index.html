How to size a trade with the Fusion calculators

November 23, 2026 · Wendling FX

Pip value, margin, profit and loss, and swaps. A one minute routine that turns a chart idea into a position you can afford to be wrong on.

Why bother

Sizing by feel gives different results from the same strategy. Fusion Markets publishes free calculators that need no login and use live conversion rates. They answer four pre-trade questions: what a pip is worth, how much capital the trade ties up, what a result would be worth, and what holding overnight costs. They sit on four tabs: Margin, Pip Value, Profit and Loss, and Swaps.

The fields that appear everywhere

Group filters the symbol list. Symbol shows the contract size under the dropdown. For XAU/USD, one lot is 100 ounces of gold. Account base currency sets the currency of the results and converts for you. Contract size is the figure people misread most often, and a lot size that is routine on EUR/USD can be far too large on gold.

The four calculators

Pip value shows what a one pip move is worth, using 1 lot as the reference. It feeds the sizing formula: position size equals risk divided by stop distance times pip value. Example: a $10,000 account risking 1% ($100), a EUR/USD entry at 1.0850 and a stop at 1.0825 (25 pips), with $10 per pip per standard lot. That gives 0.4 lots. Widen the stop to 50 pips and the size halves to 0.2 lots, with the risk unchanged.

Margin shows the equity locked up to hold the trade. Risk is what you lose if the stop is hit ($100 in the example). Margin is the capital tied up, which is much larger. A trade can be sized correctly and still cause a margin call if the account lacks free equity.

Profit and loss shows the outcome of a trade from its open and close price. Run it twice, once at the target and once at the stop, and compare the dollars. A setup risking $100 to make $80 can look attractive on a chart and poor on paper.

Swaps shows the daily overnight cost or credit for long and short separately. Direction matters, because one side can cost while the other earns. Swaps add up over multi week holds, gold and high differential pairs can carry larger charges, and most brokers charge triple on Wednesday for the weekend. If you close before rollover, you can skip this tab.

The order to use them in

Pip value at 1 lot, then the sizing formula, then margin at that size, then profit and loss at target and stop, then swaps if you hold overnight. It takes about a minute. Skip steps and you end up guessing size, being shaken out by normal noise, or taking trades whose reward does not justify the risk.

Two things to add by hand

Add the spread to your stop distance. A 25 pip stop with a 1 pip spread is really 26 pips of risk. And always round the size down: rounding 0.083 lots up to 0.09 pushes you past your risk limit. To see what a string of losses does at a given risk, read the drawdown maths.
