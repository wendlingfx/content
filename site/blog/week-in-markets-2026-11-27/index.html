Week in markets: Nvidia lifts stocks, hike bets stay

November 27, 2026 · Wendling FX

A leadership reversal at Nvidia sparked a rebound in stocks, while hot inflation data kept a December Fed hike on the table.

The short version

After last week's sell off, most risk assets bounced. The Nasdaq 100 gained 5.9%, the Dow 5.1% and the DAX 3.8%. Gold recovered 3.3%. WTI crude slipped 0.7% and bitcoin 1.8%. Currencies barely moved: EUR/USD lost 0.3%, GBP/USD added 0.1% and USD/JPY rose 0.7%.

The bounce came with a caveat. US data released midweek showed strong growth and sticky inflation, so the case for another Fed hike did not go away. For the backdrop, see last week's notes.

Nvidia turned the mood on Monday

Before Monday's open came news that Jensen Huang would return as Nvidia's chief executive, less than two weeks after he had stepped down. The stock jumped more than 31% that day and pulled the AI trade up with it. On our data the Nasdaq 100 was already 6.3% above the prior Friday's close by Monday evening.

The rest of the week was choppier. Nvidia fell on Tuesday and again on Wednesday, and the Nasdaq 100 gave back about half of Monday's gain before buyers returned. US markets were shut on Thursday for Thanksgiving and closed early on Friday, when technology led again. AP said the S&P 500 had its strongest week since April 2025. Holiday sessions are thin, which is worth knowing before you size a trade: what changes when you trade over the holidays.

Hot data, and a hike still in play

Wednesday brought a pile of US numbers. The BEA revised third quarter growth up to a 4.3% annual rate from 3.4%. In October the core PCE price index rose 0.3% on the month and 3.3% on the year, with the headline index at 3.8%. Personal income grew 0.7%, but spending adjusted for prices slipped slightly.

The Fed's Beige Book, published the same afternoon, painted a softer picture: eight of twelve districts grew slightly to moderately, four saw no change, and consumer spending was little changed. Prices rose moderately, and contacts said input costs were climbing faster than the prices they could charge. Market reports said futures still priced a December rate rise as close to certain. The 10 year Treasury yield ended Friday at 4.94%, up from 4.85% on Wednesday, according to AP.

Gold bounced, oil went nowhere

Gold slid to its lowest level in more than a year on Wednesday, then rallied into Friday. Market reports linked the rebound to a pause in forced selling as stocks calmed over the holiday and to a softer dollar after Monday's peak. Our Friday range chart shows how much ground gold still covered in a single session.

WTI dropped 4.1% on Monday, recovered midweek, and fell back on Friday, ahead of an OPEC meeting set for Sunday. US crude inventories rose for a third week in a row. Bitcoin had the widest Friday range of the nine markets we track and ended the week slightly lower.

What to watch next week

Tuesday, December 1: BLS job openings and labor turnover (JOLTS) for October.

Wednesday, December 2: EIA weekly petroleum status report.

Friday, December 4: BLS employment report for November.

Further out, the Fed meets on December 8 to 9 with new projections, and November CPI follows on December 10.
