01How the forex market works

Forex is the market where currencies are exchanged. It has no central exchange: banks, funds, companies and retail traders deal with each other through a network that runs around the clock from Monday morning in Asia to Friday evening in New York.

You always trade one currency against another. In EUR/USD the euro is the base and the dollar is the quote. A price of 1.1200 means one euro costs 1.12 dollars. If you think the euro will gain on the dollar, you buy the pair. If you think it will weaken, you sell it.

Liquidity follows the sun. The busiest hours are usually the overlap of London and New York, when both financial centres are open at the same time. Quiet hours mean wider spreads and slower moves.

PipThe standard price step. 0.0001 for most pairs, 0.01 for yen pairs. Gold and indices follow the broker's own convention.
LotTrade size. 1 standard lot = 100,000 units of the base currency. 0.01 is a micro lot.
SpreadThe gap between the buy and sell price. It is a cost you pay on every trade.
Leverage cuts both ways. With 1:100, a 1% move in price is a 100% move on the margin you put up. Leverage does not change how often you are right. It only changes how much each mistake costs.
02Reading a price chart

Most traders read price with candlesticks. Each candle covers a period of time, one hour, one day or one week, and shows four prices: where it opened, the highest and lowest points it reached, and where it closed.

A green body means the period closed higher than it opened. A red body means it closed lower. The thin lines above and below are the wicks: they show how far price travelled before being pushed back. A long wick is a sign that one side tried and failed.

Pick your timeframe on purpose

TimeframeGood forWatch out for
Weekly and dailyThe big picture, key levels, the main trendWide stops, slow feedback
4 hours and 1 hourTiming entries inside the bigger trendNoise around news
15 minutes and belowFine tuning, scalpingSpreads eat a bigger share of each move
Top down, always. Start on the daily chart to see where you are, then zoom in to decide when. A perfect 5-minute setup against the daily trend is usually a trap.
03Technical analysis: trend and structure

Technical analysis studies price itself. The first question is always the same: is the market going up, going down or going sideways?

A rising market makes higher highs and higher lows. A falling one makes lower highs and lower lows. When neither holds, the market is ranging. The chart below marks the turning points on a real gold chart: one higher high at the start, then a clean series of lower highs and lower lows as the sell-off took over.

UptrendEach pullback stops above the last one. Buyers are in control. Look for buys on pullbacks, not sells at the top.
DowntrendEach bounce fails below the last high. Sellers are in control. The first higher high is the first warning that it may be over.
Trade with the structure. Most losing trades are attempts to call the exact top or bottom. Waiting for the structure to change costs a little of the move and saves a lot of money.
04Support and resistance

Support is a price area where falling prices have stopped before. Resistance is where rising prices have stalled. These levels matter because traders remember them: orders cluster there, and so do stops.

Three rules make levels useful:

  • Zones, not lines. Price rarely turns at the exact same tick. Draw a band, not a hairline.
  • More touches, more weight. A level that held three or four times matters more than one that held once.
  • Broken levels flip. When support breaks, it often becomes resistance on the way back up, and the other way round.
Try this. Open a daily chart of any pair and mark the three most obvious turning zones of the last six months. Then zoom into the 1-hour chart and watch how price behaves when it gets back to them.
05Indicators: moving averages and RSI

Indicators are calculations made from price. They do not see the future. They help you read what price is already doing, with less noise.

Moving averages

A moving average smooths price over a number of candles. The exponential version (EMA) gives more weight to recent prices. When the fast average sits below the slow one and both point down, as in the chart above, the trend is down. Many traders use the averages as moving support or resistance.

RSI

The Relative Strength Index measures the speed of recent moves on a scale from 0 to 100. Readings above 70 mean price has risen fast, below 30 that it has fallen fast. In a strong trend RSI can stay stretched for a long time, so a low reading alone is not a reason to buy.

Less is more. Two or three indicators you understand beat ten you don't. If they disagree, price wins.

A double top or a head and shoulders shows buyers failing to make progress. The dashed line, the neckline, is the trigger: the pattern only counts once price closes through it. An ascending triangle shows sellers defending a ceiling while buyers keep pressing from higher and higher lows.

07Fundamental analysis

Fundamental analysis asks why a currency should be worth more or less. Over weeks and months, money flows towards higher returns and away from risk. Interest rates sit at the centre of that.

Inflation runs hot
→
Central bank raises rates or signals it will
→
Higher yields attract money
→
Currency tends to strengthen

The same chain works in reverse when growth weakens and rates are cut. Markets move on expectations, so a decision that everyone saw coming often changes little. The surprise is what moves price.

Gold has its own logic

Gold pays no interest, so it often struggles when real yields rise and does well when they fall. It is also priced in dollars, so a stronger dollar tends to weigh on it. In moments of stress it can act as a safe haven, but not always on the same day.

Check the calendar every morning. Know which events are due before you open a chart. Price can jump through any level in the minutes around a release.
08Risk management

Analysis decides where you might be right. Risk management decides whether you survive the times you are wrong. Professionals think about the loss first.

Size every trade from the stop

Decide how much of the account you accept to lose on one trade, then work back to the size:

Position size = (Account × risk %) ÷ (stop distance × value per pip)

08Drawdown and discipline

Every strategy goes through losing streaks. What matters is how deep they get. The chart shows the climb you face after each size of fall:

Rules that keep you in the game

  • Risk the same small share on every trade. 0.5% to 2% is the common range. Ten losses in a row at 1% cost about 10%, which you can recover from.
  • Set a daily and weekly loss limit. Hit it and stop for the day. The next trade after a big loss is the most dangerous one.
  • Never move a stop further away. Moving it closer to lock in profit is fine.
  • Do not add to a losing position to lower your average price.
  • Keep a journal. Entry, exit, reason, result, emotion. It shows your real habits within a month.
Protect the downside and the upside takes care of itself. A 50% loss needs a 100% gain just to get back to the start. Small, consistent risk is what keeps you in the game.
09Copy trading the right way

Copy trading links your own broker account to a trader's strategy. When they open a trade, your account opens the same one, scaled to your balance. On cTrader Copy your money stays in your account, in your name, and you can stop at any time.

Read the record, not the adPrefer records verified by a third party such as Myfxbook. Look at gain, drawdown, age and profit factor together. Three months says much less than twelve.
Know the feesA performance fee with a high-water mark is only paid on new highs. Management and volume fees are paid either way.
Size it sensiblyToo small and some trades cannot be copied, because brokers have a minimum lot. Too big and one bad month hurts. Use money you can afford to lose.
Leave the account aloneMoving money out while trades are open changes the scaling and can leave positions without margin. One strategy per account keeps the numbers clean.

Red flags

  • Accuracy claims with no verified account behind them
  • Profits in big dollar amounts, never in percent
  • No drawdown anywhere on the page
  • Pressure to message an admin in private for the price
  • Records that restart again and again
10Your checklist

Before any trade

  • What is the structure on the daily chart?
  • Where are the nearest support and resistance zones?
  • Is there a high-impact event in the next hours?
  • Where is my stop, and what size does it give me at my risk %?
  • Is the reward at least twice the risk?

Before copying anyone

  • Is the record verified and at least three months old?
  • What was the worst drawdown, and could I live with it?
  • What exactly are the fees, and when are they charged?
  • Does my balance clear the minimum with room to spare?
